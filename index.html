IN PROGRESS · 2026

Hybrid neuro-symbolic control for RL in AirSim

My current research asks a practical question: a policy network can learn to fly, but can it explain or respect a rule? I am pairing a learned controller with an explicit symbolic layer — predicates, safety envelopes and mission constraints — inside a delivery fixed-wing UAV simulation built on AirSim.

The loop is deliberately split: the neural side proposes continuous actions from a compact observation vector, the symbolic side verifies and, where needed, amends them before they reach the flight model. The aim is a controller that keeps the sample efficiency of deep RL and the auditability of a rule base — and one that can be interrogated when it does something strange.

№ 005 ABOUT

Learning by doing

Passionate AI/ML developer with a focus on computer vision, intelligent systems and real-world problem solving. I believe in learning by doing and embrace the journey of continuous improvement.

I work with cutting-edge technologies including Rust-based ML frameworks like Burn, traditional Python ML stacks, and full-stack development tools. My goal is to build intelligent systems that make a real impact.

My published research — a Rust-implemented Dueling Double DQN for autonomous fixed-wing flight in JAMCS (2026) — is exactly the kind of problem I love: physics, reinforcement learning, and a game engine (Godot 4 through GDExtension) all wired together and documented end to end.

That thread continues in my current work: a hybrid neuro-symbolic controller for reinforcement-learning agents flying a delivery UAV in AirSim, where a learned policy and an explicit rule base have to agree before anything moves.

Outside the terminal I'm into flight sims, tinkering with autonomous drones, and pushing browser visualisation far past "good enough". I break things on purpose to learn faster, and I always leave a trail of notes behind.
